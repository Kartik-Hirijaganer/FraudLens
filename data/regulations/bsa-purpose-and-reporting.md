title: Purpose of the Bank Secrecy Act and required reports and records
citation: 31 U.S.C. 5311
source: FinCEN / Bank Secrecy Act
---

It is the declared purpose of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) to require certain reports or
records where they have a high degree of usefulness in criminal, tax, or regulatory
investigations or proceedings, or in the conduct of intelligence or counterintelligence
activities, including analysis, to protect against international terrorism and money
laundering.

Financial institutions must establish and maintain a risk-based anti-money-laundering
(AML) program reasonably designed to prevent the institution from being used to facilitate
money laundering or the financing of terrorism. The program includes internal controls,
independent testing, a designated compliance officer, ongoing training, and customer due
diligence.

Reports and records required under the BSA include currency transaction reports, reports
of suspicious activity, records of funds transfers, and records relating to the identity of
customers and beneficial owners. These obligations support detection of structuring, layering,
and the movement of illicit proceeds through the financial system.
