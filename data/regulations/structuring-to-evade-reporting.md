---
docId: structuring-to-evade-reporting
title: Structuring transactions to evade reporting requirements is prohibited
citation: 31 CFR 1010.314
source: FinCEN / Bank Secrecy Act
---

No person shall, for the purpose of evading the currency transaction reporting requirements,
structure or assist in structuring, or attempt to structure or assist in structuring, any
transaction with one or more financial institutions. The statutory prohibition at 31 U.S.C.
5324 makes structuring unlawful without regard to whether an individual transaction is itself
reportable.

Structuring occurs when a person, acting alone or with others, conducts or attempts to conduct
a series of currency transactions in amounts below the $10,000 reporting threshold so that no
single Currency Transaction Report is triggered. Typical patterns include breaking a large cash
deposit into several smaller deposits, making repeated round-amount deposits just under the
threshold, spreading deposits across multiple branches or days, and using multiple accounts or
intermediaries to disguise the aggregate amount.

Indicators that warrant review include cash deposits or withdrawals consistently just below
$10,000, round-dollar amounts repeated over a short period, and an account whose deposit pattern
changes to avoid triggering a report. Such conduct is reportable as suspicious activity.
