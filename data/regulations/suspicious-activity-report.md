docId: suspicious-activity-report
title: Reports of suspicious transactions (Suspicious Activity Report)
citation: 31 U.S.C. 5318(g)
source: FinCEN / Bank Secrecy Act
---

A financial institution, and any director, officer, employee, or agent of the institution,
may be required to report any suspicious transaction relevant to a possible violation of law
or regulation. Implementing rules require a financial institution to file a Suspicious
Activity Report (SAR) when it detects a known or suspected violation of federal law, or a
suspicious transaction related to money laundering activity or a violation of the Bank Secrecy
Act, and the transaction involves or aggregates to the applicable dollar threshold.

A transaction is suspicious if the institution knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that
it involves funds derived from illegal activity; is designed to evade Bank Secrecy Act
requirements, including through structuring; has no apparent lawful purpose or is not the sort
of transaction the customer would normally be expected to engage in; or uses the institution to
facilitate criminal activity.

The SAR must describe the who, what, when, where, and why of the suspicious activity in a clear
and complete narrative. A financial institution and its personnel are prohibited from disclosing
to any person involved in the transaction that a SAR has been filed (the confidentiality, or "no
tipping off", rule), and are afforded a safe harbor from civil liability for the report.
