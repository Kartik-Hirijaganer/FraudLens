docId: funds-transfer-recordkeeping
title: Recordkeeping and the Travel Rule for funds and wire transfers
citation: 31 CFR 1010.410
source: FinCEN / Bank Secrecy Act
---

Financial institutions must retain records for funds transfers and transmittals of funds in
amounts of $3,000 or more, including wire transfers. For each qualifying transmittal of funds,
the transmittor's financial institution must obtain and retain the name and address of the
transmittor, the amount, the execution date, the payment instructions, and the identity of the
recipient's financial institution.

Under the Travel Rule, the transmittor's financial institution must include this originator and
beneficiary information and pass it on to the next financial institution in the payment chain.
Intermediary and beneficiary institutions must in turn retain the information they receive. The
rule is designed to create a transparent audit trail that follows cross-border and domestic wire
transfers through correspondent and intermediary banks.

Wire and other electronic funds transfers that lack complete originator or beneficiary
information, that are routed through multiple intermediaries to obscure the source, or that move
funds rapidly in and out of an account are recognized layering techniques and may warrant a
suspicious activity report.
